# **6️⃣ Real‑World Example — Illegal Migration Across the English Channel**

---

# 🧱 **Introduction**
This document provides a real‑world example showing how **static law** and **polymorphic law** behave differently when applied to the same scenario.

The example focuses on **illegal migration across the English Channel**, because it clearly demonstrates:

- how static law creates long‑term loopholes  
- how polymorphic law allows the electorate to adapt definitions  
- how both models produce different outcomes without changing the mission of the law  

This page is written in the same style as the rest of the framework:  
clear, neutral, structural, and accessible to any reader.

---

# **1️⃣ Scenario Under Static Law**

## 🔁 **Static Law Behaviour**
Under the current static legal structure:

- organised groups facilitate illegal crossings  
- migrants are placed in dangerous situations  
- lawyers challenge deportation using fixed definitions  
- loopholes remain because definitions cannot be updated quickly  
- the environment changes faster than the legislation  

Static law embeds definitions inside the legislation.  
This means:

> **Even if the electorate votes for a party that promises to reduce illegal migration, the law itself cannot adapt quickly enough to close the loopholes being exploited.**

### **Why This Happens**
- definitions are fixed  
- thresholds are fixed  
- conditions are fixed  
- assumptions are fixed  
- updates require rewriting the entire law  
- rewriting takes months or years  
- lawyers rely on these fixed definitions to challenge deportation  

The result is a structural mismatch:

> **Static law is slow, but the environment is fast.**

---

# **2️⃣ Scenario Under Polymorphic Law**

## 🌱 **Polymorphic Law Behaviour**
Under polymorphic law, the electorate does not vote for a party promising to “stop illegal migration.”

Instead, the electorate votes to **adapt the definitions** that are currently being exploited.

This is the key difference.

### **What Happens Next**
Because definitions are externalised:

- the government instructs the **House of Experts**  
- to rewrite the definitions associated with illegal migration  
- under the authority of the electorate  
- without rewriting the law itself  

This can be done **immediately**, because:

> **Changing a definition is not changing the law — it is updating the environment the law refers to.**

### **Effect on Legal Challenges**
Lawyers are now bound by the updated definitions.

This means:

- loopholes close  
- outdated assumptions are removed  
- cases become less lucrative  
- fewer challenges succeed  
- enforcement aligns with the electorate’s mandate  

The mission of the law does not change.  
The structure of the law does not change.  
Only the **definitions** change — and they change under democratic authority.

---

# **3️⃣ Side‑by‑Side Comparison**

| Aspect | Static Law | Polymorphic Law |
|-------|------------|-----------------|
| **Definitions** | Fixed inside the law | Externalised and adaptable |
| **Update Speed** | Slow (requires rewriting law) | Fast (definitions updated directly) |
| **Electorate Power** | Votes for parties | Votes to adapt definitions |
| **Government Action** | Limited by fixed definitions | Can update definitions immediately |
| **Lawyer Strategy** | Exploit fixed definitions | Bound by updated definitions |
| **Outcome** | Loopholes persist | Loopholes close |

---

# **4️⃣ Summary — The Difference in One Sentence**

## 🔁 **Static Law**
> Illegal migration continues because definitions cannot be updated quickly enough to close loopholes.

## 🌱 **Polymorphic Law**
> Illegal migration is reduced because definitions can be updated immediately under the authority of the electorate.

---

# **5️⃣ Why This Example Matters**
This example shows the core strength of polymorphic law:

> **The mission of the law stays the same, but the definitions evolve to match the environment.**

Static law cannot adapt fast enough.  
Polymorphic law is designed to adapt continuously.

This is the structural difference that makes the polymorphic model more practical in fast‑changing real‑world scenarios.
