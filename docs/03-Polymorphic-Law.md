# **3️⃣ Polymorphic Law**

---

# **🧱 Introduction**
This document explains how **polymorphic law** operates as an adaptive alternative to the current static legal model described in Document 02.  
Its purpose is not to criticise existing lawmaking, but to show how the **polymorphic principle** can be applied to create legislation that remains aligned with a changing environment.

This page mirrors the structure of **02 – Current Static Law**, allowing readers to compare both models side‑by‑side.

---

# **1️⃣ The Polymorphic Law Lifecycle**

Polymorphic law follows a **layered, adaptive sequence**.  
Each stage assumes the **environment will continue to evolve**, and the legal framework is designed to evolve with it.

---

## **🌱 Step 1 — A Problem Is Identified**
A societal, technological, economic, or organisational issue emerges that requires formal resolution.

But unlike static law, polymorphic law assumes:

> **The problem and environment will continue to change during and after the legislative process.**

This assumption shapes the entire lifecycle.

---

## **📝 Step 2 — Law Is Drafted Using Constants**
Instead of embedding fixed definitions inside the law, the legislation is written using **constants** — stable statements that do not change.

Examples:
- “It is illegal to exceed the defined speed limit.”  
- “A business must comply with the defined safety standard.”  
- “A citizen must meet the defined eligibility criteria.”

These constants form the **stable backbone** of the legal system.

---

## **🔬 Step 3 — Definitions Are Externalised**
Definitions are stored outside the law in a **Definitions Layer**.

Examples:
- “Speed limit = 30mph in residential zones.”  
- “Safety standard = ISO‑XXXX.”  
- “Eligibility criteria = income threshold + residency requirement.”

These definitions can be updated **without rewriting the law**.

## **🔁 Step 4 — Definitions Are Updated Regularly**
Definitions are updated:

- annually  
- quarterly  
- after elections  
- when new evidence emerges  
- when technology changes  
- when environmental conditions shift  

Updates are performed by a **House of Experts**, authorised by the electorate and overseen by government.

This ensures:
- the law stays aligned with reality  
- loopholes close automatically  
- outdated assumptions are removed  
- enforcement remains consistent  

---

## **⚖️ Step 5 — Law Remains Evergreen**
Because definitions adapt, the law never becomes historic.

This solves the structural limitation described in Document 02:

> **Static law is drafted for the past, implemented in the present, and applied to the future.  
> Polymorphic law is drafted for the present and remains aligned with the future.**

---

# **2️⃣ Why Polymorphic Law Is More Practical**

## **🧩 Structural Advantages**
Polymorphic law provides:

- **faster adaptation**  
- **fewer loopholes**  
- **shorter legislation**  
- **clearer language**  
- **consistent enforcement**  
- **future‑proof governance**  

It does not replace democracy.  
It enhances it by making law responsive to the environment the electorate lives in.

# **🎯 Purpose of This Document**
This document provides a clear, domain‑neutral explanation of how polymorphic law works.

Its purpose is to show:

> **Polymorphic law keeps the legal structure stable while allowing the operational meaning to evolve.**

This resolves the mismatch between:
- static law (slow, fixed)  
- real‑world environments (fast, dynamic)
