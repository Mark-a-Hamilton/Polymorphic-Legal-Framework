# **5️⃣ Implementation**

---

# 🧱 **Introduction**
This document explains how a government in waiting could implement **polymorphic law** despite the major obstacle described in the Static Law document:

> **A single static law can take months or years to be ratified.**

If this were true for polymorphic law, the new structure could never be implemented within a single term — or even within a realistic timeframe.

But there is a solution.

Polymorphic law does **not** require new laws.  
It requires a new **format** for existing laws.

This changes everything.

---

# **1️⃣ The Obstruction: Static Law Takes Too Long to Ratify**

Under the current system:

- drafting takes months  
- committee review takes months  
- Commons ↔ Lords “ping‑pong” takes months or years  
- amendments restart the cycle  
- political delays extend the timeline  
- ratification is slow and unpredictable  

This means:

> **A government cannot realistically rewrite the entire legal system within a single term.**

If polymorphic law required *new* laws, the project would be impossible.

---

# **2️⃣ The Breakthrough: Polymorphic Laws Are Not New Laws**

A polymorphic law is **not** a new piece of legislation.

It is:

> **A direct structural rewrite of an existing law that has already been ratified.**

This means:

- the content stays the same  
- the mission stays the same  
- the legal effect stays the same  
- the authority stays the same  

Only the **format** changes.

The law is rewritten using:

- **constants** (stable backbone)  
- **external definitions** (adaptive layer)

Because the law’s *meaning* does not change, the rewritten version does **not** require ratification.

It is legally identical — but structurally modernised.

---

# **3️⃣ What This Achieves (Even Before Adaptation Begins)**

Rewriting static laws into polymorphic format does **not** solve loopholes immediately.

It does **not** fix outdated definitions instantly.

It does **not** modernise the environment overnight.

But it *does* achieve something foundational:

> **It gives the electorate the power to adapt definitions through general elections.**

This is the core democratic upgrade.

Once the law is polymorphic:

- definitions can be updated  
- updates can be authorised by the electorate  
- updates can be implemented by experts  
- updates can be scheduled  
- updates can be continuous  

The law becomes **adaptable**, even if the initial rewrite is a direct copy.

---

# **4️⃣ Why This Works: The Environment Is Moved Out of the Law**

Static law embeds the environment inside the legislation:

- definitions  
- thresholds  
- categories  
- conditions  
- assumptions  

When the environment changes, the law becomes historic.

Polymorphic law removes the environment from the legislation and places it in a **Definitions Layer**.

This means:

- the law stays stable  
- the definitions evolve  
- the environment is always current  
- loopholes close automatically  
- enforcement stays aligned  
- updates no longer require rewriting the law  

This is the structural breakthrough that makes implementation possible.

---

# **5️⃣ Implementation in Practice**

A government in waiting could implement polymorphic law by:

### **Step 1 — Copying Existing Laws**
Every static law is rewritten into polymorphic format:

- same mission  
- same content  
- same legal effect  
- same authority  
- same scope  

No ratification required.

### **Step 2 — Externalising Definitions**
Definitions are moved into the Definitions Layer:

- thresholds  
- categories  
- conditions  
- assumptions  
- parameters  

Still no ratification required.

### **Step 3 — Establishing the Update Mechanism**
The electorate approves the update mechanism through general elections.

### **Step 4 — Beginning Scheduled Updates**
Experts maintain definitions.  
The electorate authorises changes.  
The law stays aligned with reality.

---

# **6️⃣ Summary — The Implementation in One Sentence**

> **Polymorphic law can be implemented quickly because it is not new law — it is a new format for existing law.**

This allows:

- fast deployment  
- democratic adaptation  
- expert maintenance  
- future‑proof governance  

Without waiting years for ratification.
