# **16️⃣❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)**

## **📘 Purpose of This Document**  
This FAQ provides clear, plain‑English answers to the most common questions about the **Polymorphic Legal Framework**, explaining how missions, definitions, experts, elections, and Parliament work together to create a modern, efficient, citizen‑focused legal system.

---

# **❓ Frequently Asked Questions**

---

## **1️⃣ What is a Polymorphic Law?**  
A polymorphic law is a law with **two components**:

- **Mission (constant)** — the expert‑defined purpose of the law  
- **Definitions (adaptive)** — the electorate‑driven environmental parameters  

This separation allows laws to remain **stable** while adapting quickly to societal changes.

---

## **2️⃣ How does this differ from static law?**  
Static laws:

- take years to update  
- become historic  
- rely on political negotiation  
- often contradict each other  

Polymorphic laws:

- update definitions quickly  
- remain modern  
- avoid contradictions  
- focus on citizen needs, not ideology  

---

## **3️⃣ Who decides the missions of each law?**  
Missions are defined by **subject‑matter experts** in the House of Experts.  
They ensure each law:

- serves the UK  
- is efficient  
- is enforceable  
- aligns with the wider legal framework  

---

## **4️⃣ Who decides the definitions?**  
Definitions are driven by the **electorate**, either:

- **directly** through elections  
- **indirectly** when contradictions block implementation  

Experts update definitions based on the electorate’s mandate.

---

## **5️⃣ How do elections influence the legal system?**  
Elections are **information‑gathering exercises**.  
Citizens choose the manifestos that best represent their needs.  
The winning manifestos are:

- unedited  
- passed directly to Parliament  
- forwarded to the House of Experts  

This ensures the legal system reflects **real‑world needs**, not political ideology.

---

## **6️⃣ What is the role of Parliament?**  
Parliament:

- receives unedited manifestos  
- shortlists the most popular requirements  
- passes them to the House of Experts  
- resolves contradictions that block implementation  

Parliament ensures the framework remains **democratic and constitutional**.

---

## **7️⃣ What does the House of Experts actually do?**  
Experts:

- perform Root Cause Analysis  
- identify upstream definitions  
- propose minimum changes with maximum impact  
- maintain mission integrity  
- ensure laws work together as a framework  

They are the **technical engine** of the system.

---

## **8️⃣ What are upstream definitions?**  
Upstream definitions are the **highest‑impact** parts of the legal environment.  
Updating one upstream definition can:

- modernise multiple laws  
- solve multiple problems  
- prevent contradictions  
- improve efficiency across the framework  

---

## **9️⃣ What happens if a mission cannot be implemented?**  
If a mission contradicts existing legislation or is unjustifiable:

- it is temporarily omitted  
- the reason is documented  
- Parliament is notified  
- MPs are incentivised to fix the contradiction  

This prevents multi‑year delays and keeps the system moving.

---

## **🔟 How does the framework stay modern?**  
Definitions are updated regularly based on:

- elections  
- societal changes  
- environmental changes  
- technological changes  
- expert analysis  

This ensures the legal system is **never older than five years**.

---

## **1️⃣1️⃣ How does this prevent contradictions?**  
Experts maintain **Mission Integrity** by:

- linking laws through shared Acts  
- updating upstream definitions  
- checking the entire framework before approving changes  

This ensures laws evolve **together**, not independently.

---

## **1️⃣2️⃣ Why is this better for the electorate?**  
Because the system:

- focuses on citizen needs  
- removes political ideology  
- updates quickly  
- prevents outdated laws  
- ensures consistent enforcement  
- delivers improvements faster  

The electorate becomes the **primary driver** of legal evolution.
